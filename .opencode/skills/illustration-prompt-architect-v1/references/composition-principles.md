# Composition Principles for Anime Illustration

## Camera Angles and Risk Assessment

### Safe Angles (High Success Rate)

**Eye Level (水平视角)**
- Camera at character's eye height
- Most reliable for full-body and half-body shots
- Minimal perspective distortion
- Best for: character focus, emotional connection, dialogue scenes

**Slight High Angle (轻微俯视, 10-20°)**
- Camera slightly above character
- Creates gentle, approachable feeling
- Still maintains good facial visibility
- Best for: cute/moe aesthetics, vulnerable moments, indoor scenes

**Slight Low Angle (轻微仰视, 10-20°)**
- Camera slightly below character
- Adds dignity, confidence, or drama
- Requires attention to chin/neck area
- Best for: heroic poses, confident characters, outdoor scenes

### Medium Risk Angles (Requires Careful Constraints)

**Medium High Angle (中度俯视, 30-45°)**
- Shows more of top of head and shoulders
- Character appears smaller, more vulnerable
- Risk: perspective mismatch between character and background
- Mitigation: Explicitly state character foreshortening must match background perspective

**Medium Low Angle (中度仰视, 30-45°)**
- Emphasizes character height and presence
- Risk: chin/neck distortion, nostril visibility
- Mitigation: Specify "face remains well-proportioned, no extreme foreshortening of facial features"

### High Risk Angles (Often Fails)

**Extreme High Angle (极度俯视, 60°+)**
- Bird's eye view
- Risk: Character becomes flat cutout, perspective collapse, limb distortion
- Alternative: Use 35-45° instead, or switch to half-body framing

**Extreme Low Angle (极度仰视, 60°+)**
- Worm's eye view
- Risk: Face distortion, unnatural body proportions, background-character mismatch
- Alternative: Use 30-40° with careful facial proportion constraints

**Dutch Angle (荷兰角/倾斜构图)**
- Tilted horizon line
- Risk: Adds complexity that compounds with other perspective challenges
- Use only with simple, eye-level base perspective

## Composition Patterns

### Rule of Thirds (三分法则)

Place key elements (eyes, hands, focal objects) at intersection points of a 3x3 grid.

**When to use:**
- Single character portraits
- Character + environment storytelling
- Emphasizing specific emotional elements

**Implementation:**
"Position the character's face in the upper-right third of the frame, with her eyes at the intersection point. The window occupies the left third, creating negative space that balances the composition."

### Center Composition (中心构图)

Subject placed in center, often with symmetrical framing.

**When to use:**
- Formal portraits
- Powerful/authoritative characters
- Symmetrical architectural settings
- Frontal character designs

**Risk:** Can feel static if not balanced with dynamic pose or asymmetric background elements.

### Diagonal Composition (对角线构图)

Key elements arranged along diagonal lines.

**When to use:**
- Action scenes
- Dynamic movement
- Creating visual flow
- Guiding viewer's eye through the image

**Implementation:**
"The character's body forms a diagonal line from upper-left to lower-right, with her extended arm continuing the line toward the bottom corner. Background architectural elements echo this diagonal, creating unified visual flow."

### Foreground Framing (前景框架)

Using foreground elements to frame the main subject.

**When to use:**
- Adding depth
- Creating intimacy or voyeuristic feeling
- Environmental storytelling

**Common elements:** Tree branches, doorways, window frames, curtains, flowers

**Risk:** Foreground can compete with subject if too detailed. Keep foreground soft/out-of-focus.

## Spatial Depth Techniques

### Three-Plane Composition (三层空间)

Organize elements into foreground, midground, and background.

**Foreground (前景):**
- Soft focus, high contrast
- Framing elements, atmospheric particles
- 10-20% of visual weight

**Midground (中景):**
- Sharp focus, main subject
- Character and immediate environment
- 60-70% of visual weight

**Background (背景):**
- Reduced detail, atmospheric perspective
- Context and mood setting
- 10-30% of visual weight

### Atmospheric Perspective (空气透视)

Distant elements become:
- Lower contrast
- Cooler/bluer in color (unless warm atmospheric conditions)
- Less detailed
- Softer edges

**Implementation:**
"Background buildings fade into cool blue-gray tones with soft edges and minimal detail. Midground trees retain local color but with reduced saturation. Foreground flowers show full color saturation and sharp edges."

## Common Scene Types and Optimal Approaches

### Indoor Room Scene (室内场景)

**Recommended angle:** Eye level or slight high angle (10-25°)
**Why:** Indoor spaces have many perspective cues (walls, floor, furniture). High angles multiply perspective complexity.

**Safe setup:**
- Character positioned away from walls (reduces perspective conflict)
- Simple furniture arrangement
- Single dominant light source
- Clear ground plane definition

**Risky setup:**
- High angle + full body + complex furniture
- Multiple windows with different perspective vanishing points
- Character interacting with furniture (sitting on chair, leaning on desk)

### Outdoor Landscape (户外风景)

**Recommended angle:** Eye level to medium high angle (0-35°)
**Why:** Natural environments are more forgiving of perspective variations.

**Safe setup:**
- Character in open space (field, beach, plaza)
- Simple horizon line
- Natural framing elements (trees, rocks)

**Risky setup:**
- Complex architectural background
- Multiple depth planes with detailed elements
- Reflective surfaces (water, glass buildings)

### Character Interaction (角色互动)

**Recommended angle:** Eye level or slight angle matching emotional dynamic
**Why:** Two characters double the anatomical complexity.

**Safe setup:**
- Clear spatial relationship (who's in front, distance between)
- Simple poses (standing, sitting side-by-side)
- Unified lighting on both characters

**Risky setup:**
- Overlapping bodies (hugging, fighting)
- Complex hand interaction (holding hands, touching faces)
- Different height characters with extreme angle

### Close-up Portrait (特写肖像)

**Recommended angle:** Any angle works if face-only
**Why:** No body or environment to create perspective conflicts.

**Safe setup:**
- Shoulders and up
- Simple or blurred background
- Focus on facial expression

**Note:** This is the most forgiving composition type. Use it when other approaches seem too risky.

## Composition Troubleshooting

### Problem: Character looks pasted onto background

**Cause:** Perspective mismatch - character drawn in one perspective, background in another

**Solution:**
- Add explicit constraint: "Character foreshortening must match background perspective"
- Simplify background to reduce perspective cues
- Add contact shadows where character meets ground
- Include environmental interaction (character touching wall, sitting on furniture)

### Problem: Image feels flat despite depth cues

**Cause:** Uniform detail level across all depth planes

**Solution:**
- Specify detail hierarchy: "Foreground elements soft, character sharp, background simplified"
- Add atmospheric perspective: "Background 30% lower saturation, cooler temperature"
- Include overlapping elements to establish depth

### Problem: Composition feels unbalanced

**Cause:** Visual weight not distributed according to intent

**Solution:**
- Identify the focal point (usually character's face or hands)
- Balance with negative space or secondary elements
- Use lighting to guide attention: "Brightest area is character's face, background 40% darker"

### Problem: Too much empty space or too crowded

**Cause:** Framing doesn't match subject size

**Solution:**
- Adjust framing: full-body needs more space, close-up needs less
- Add environmental elements to fill space purposefully
- Use negative space intentionally for emotional effect (isolation, freedom, contemplation)

## Quick Reference: Angle Selection

| Scene Type | Recommended Angle | Avoid |
|------------|------------------|-------|
| Indoor full-body | Eye level, slight high (0-20°) | High angle (45°+) |
| Outdoor full-body | Eye level to medium high (0-35°) | Extreme angles |
| Character interaction | Eye level | High/low angles |
| Portrait/close-up | Any angle | N/A (most forgiving) |
| Action/dynamic | Slight low to medium low (15-40°) | Extreme high |
| Cute/moe aesthetic | Slight high (10-25°) | Low angles |
| Heroic/powerful | Slight to medium low (15-35°) | High angles |
| Sitting/reclining | Eye level or slight high | Extreme angles |

## Integration with Other Layers

Composition decisions affect all other prompt layers:

- **Lighting:** Angle determines where light naturally falls
- **Anatomy:** Angle determines which body parts need foreshortening constraints
- **Background:** Angle determines perspective complexity
- **Emotion:** Angle reinforces or contradicts emotional intent

Always consider composition first, then build other layers to support it.
